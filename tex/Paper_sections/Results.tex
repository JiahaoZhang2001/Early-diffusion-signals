This section reports the end-to-end results from the updated early-window pipeline using (i) residualized structural shape descriptors, (ii) Hawkes-style dynamic parameters, and (iii) structure--tempo interaction terms. The organization follows the final design of the thesis. I first report the main FibVID results under the size-based $k$-window specification, which is the primary empirical setting. I then compare those results to the original Twitter15/16 benchmark, add a supplementary representation check for veracity, and finally report time-window robustness results. This ordering is meant to answer the two main research questions directly: whether early diffusion is informative for eventual reach, and whether it is similarly informative for veracity.

\subsection{FibVID Main Results: Answering the Two Core Questions}
Figure~\ref{fig:k_veracity_main} and Figure~\ref{fig:k_reach_main} show the full trajectory across $k$ for FibVID veracity (AUC) and reach ($R^2$). Here, \emph{structure-only} denotes residualized structural shape features only (no tempo, dynamics, or interaction terms): this ablation isolates structural incremental signal and helps avoid treating volume effects as structure effects. At each fixed window, the gaps baseline$\rightarrow$structure-only and structure-only$\rightarrow$full/interaction-full are interpreted as incremental value from structure, then from added dynamics/interactions. The horizontal axis is window size $k$ and the vertical axis is performance (AUC or $R^2$, higher is better); colored lines are feature bundles and error bars are 95\% CIs from SE over fixed CV folds.

The main pattern is asymmetric across tasks, and this answers the two core questions of the thesis differently. For eventual reach, the answer is clearly yes: on FibVID, early diffusion is strongly informative. Even simple baseline models perform well, and full bundles remain high throughout the larger windows, with OLS reaching $R^2 \approx 0.886$ at $k=180$ and the interaction-full specification peaking at about $R^2=0.861$ at $k=120$. For veracity, the answer is much more qualified. Veracity performance is modest throughout. The strongest baseline logit result appears late rather than early, reaching AUC $\approx 0.591$ at $k=180$, while richer bundles do not improve consistently over that baseline. Table~\ref{tab:k_main_selected} reports selected baseline-vs-full comparisons at representative $k$ windows.

This split matters substantively, not just descriptively. Much of the earlier literature established that false and true content can look different once full cascades are observed, especially in retrospective studies such as \citet{vosoughi_spread_2018}. The FibVID results show that this does not automatically translate into strong \emph{early} classifiability. Early diffusion geometry appears to carry much more recoverable information about how far a cascade will spread than about whether the underlying claim is false. That is the first main empirical answer of the thesis.

For veracity, observed AUC is 0.547 versus a null mean of 0.500, but the empirical $p$-value is 0.195. This is consistent with the earlier reading of the main figures: on FibVID, diffusion features are much more convincing for reach than for veracity. Put differently, the evidence level is not symmetric across the two research questions. The reach result is statistically and substantively strong, whereas the veracity result is weaker and should be interpreted more cautiously.

\subsection{Comparative Benchmark: Twitter15/16}
The Twitter15/16 benchmark helps show which parts of the pattern carry over from the original rumor-tree setting. The basic asymmetry remains. Under the benchmark, the best interaction-full veracity result reaches AUC $\approx 0.657$ at $k=20$, while interaction-full reach reaches $R^2 \approx 0.719$ at $k=180$. Model-family comparisons in the benchmark also point in the same broad direction as FibVID: logit remains competitive for veracity in earlier windows, whereas tree-based regressors are stronger for reach, especially at larger $k$.

Relative to Twitter15/16, FibVID weakens the veracity side but strengthens the reach side. Put differently, the early-diffusion signal for reach appears stable and in this case even stronger on FibVID, whereas the signal for veracity remains modest in both datasets and is weaker on FibVID. The permutation tests show the same contrast. In Twitter15/16 at $k=60$, observed veracity AUC is about 0.640 against a null mean of 0.499, while observed reach $R^2$ is about 0.254 against a null mean of $-0.302$ (both empirical $p=0/200$). On FibVID, reach still clearly separates from the null, but veracity does not do so as strongly.

This comparison matters for interpretation. The Twitter benchmark shows that the original pipeline was capable of extracting some early veracity signal in a clean native tree setting. The FibVID results, however, make clear that this signal is limited and does not generalize as strongly as the reach result. This directly sharpens the thesis response to the literature. Findings such as \citet{vosoughi_spread_2018} are clearly relevant, but the present comparison suggests that full-cascade diffusion differences by veracity do not straightforwardly imply a strong early-warning veracity classifier. By contrast, the reach result is much more stable across the benchmark and the updated main dataset. That is why the final interpretation of veracity is more cautious: it is better read as a weak screening signal than as a strong classifier.

\subsection{Representation Sensitivity for Veracity}
\paragraph{Supplementary representation check for veracity.}
As a supplementary check, I also estimate veracity models on a native graph representation of FibVID that does not force claims into a single-root cascade. In that specification, the best veracity result reaches AUC $\approx 0.616$ at $W=180$. I treat this as a representation check rather than as a second main pipeline. It suggests that veracity may benefit from retaining more of the original claim-level topology, but it does not alter the main result of the thesis, which is based on the harmonized cascade-compatible representation. I do not use the native graph setup as the main reach specification, because the reach question in this thesis is tied more directly to a cascade-compatible full-cascade design and to cross-dataset comparability with the original benchmark.

\subsection{Time-Window Robustness}
The time-window trajectory remains useful as a secondary diagnostic (Figure~\ref{fig:time_learning_curve}), especially for showing whether the main conclusions depend on the choice of window definition. On FibVID, the time-window results preserve the same qualitative ordering as the size-based analysis: veracity remains modest, while reach is still more predictable than veracity. The best time-window veracity result is around AUC $=0.570$ at $T=10$ under the interaction-full bundle, while the best full-bundle veracity result is around AUC $=0.545$ at $T=90$. For reach, the best full-bundle time-window result reaches about $R^2=0.396$ at $T=180$. This is far below the best size-based result on FibVID ($R^2 \approx 0.886$ at $k=180$), which supports keeping size-based windows as the main specification.

The same design logic also remains reasonable on the Twitter15/16 side. In the earlier benchmark analysis, time-window results were useful as a supplementary check on whether the main pattern depended on fixed-message windows alone, but they did not replace the size-based design as the primary specification. Taken together, the two datasets point in the same direction: time windows remain informative as a robustness check, but $k$-windows remain better suited to the main analysis because they hold observed information quantity fixed and recover much stronger reach signal.

\paragraph{Summary.}
Across tasks, the revised pipeline supports three conclusions. First, the answer to the reach question is clearly positive: early diffusion is strongly informative for eventual cascade size, especially under the harmonized size-based design. Second, the answer to the veracity question is more limited: early diffusion carries some signal, but not enough to justify a strong classification reading, and the most defensible interpretation is weak early screening. Third, Twitter15/16 remain useful as a benchmark for interpreting these findings, but the main substantive result is now anchored in FibVID rather than in the older rumor-tree dataset.
