This thesis asked whether early diffusion signals can help predict two different outcomes under an early-warning constraint: eventual reach and veracity. The answer is not symmetric across the two tasks. Across FibVID and the Twitter15/16 benchmark, early diffusion is substantially more informative for eventual reach than for veracity. In the main FibVID analysis under the harmonized size-based specification, reach prediction is strong, while veracity remains only modestly above chance. The Twitter15/16 benchmark shows the same broad ordering, although veracity is somewhat stronger there. Taken together, the results do not support the claim that early diffusion can classify truth reliably. They do support a narrower but still useful conclusion: early diffusion contains weak misinformation-risk signal and much stronger information about likely cascade scale.

This is the main substantive contribution of the thesis. Much of the earlier misinformation literature showed that false and true content can diffuse differently when full cascades are observed retrospectively. The present thesis refines that claim by putting the problem under explicit early-observation constraints. Under those constraints, veracity-linked diffusion differences are not absent, but they are much harder to recover than reach-related differences. In other words, the descriptive literature on full-cascade differences remains relevant, but it overstates what can be expected from very early diffusion alone if the practical task is real-time prediction. The strongest and most stable result in this thesis is therefore not early truth classification, but early reach forecasting.

The thesis also contributes methodologically. First, it shows why residualizing structural features against early volume matters. Without that step, successful prediction can too easily collapse into a restatement of early size. Second, it shows that window definition matters. Size-based $k$-windows remain the strongest main specification because they hold observed information quantity fixed, while time-window results are weaker and are best interpreted as robustness checks. Third, it shows that structural representation matters. The harmonized cascade-compatible representation is the most defensible common framework for the main FibVID/Twitter comparison, but the supplementary native-graph result suggests that veracity is more representation-sensitive than reach. These are not just implementation details. They shape what kind of early diffusion signal can actually be recovered.

The practical implication is a staged view of diffusion-based early warning. For veracity, the most defensible use of diffusion-only features is screening or triage. They may help identify which cascades deserve earlier verification attention, but they are not strong enough here to justify confident truth judgments on their own. For reach, the case is much stronger. As more early interactions become visible, structural and dynamic features provide a useful basis for prioritizing likely high-impact cascades. In that sense, early diffusion is better suited to helping allocate monitoring and response capacity than to replacing downstream fact-checking.

These conclusions remain bounded. The estimates are predictive rather than causal, they depend on explicit window definitions, and they are partly conditional on how propagation structure is represented in the available data. The released datasets also capture visible interaction traces rather than the full exposure environment in which misinformation spreads. Even with those limits, however, the main empirical message is clear. Early diffusion is much better at indicating \emph{how far} a cascade may go than \emph{whether} it is false.

Future work should build directly on that asymmetry rather than assume a single diffusion signal serves both tasks equally well. One direction is to combine early diffusion with content or source-level features, especially for veracity. Another is to test whether the same task split appears in newer or platform-specific propagation settings. A third is to develop more native representations for datasets such as FibVID while keeping enough comparability to support cross-dataset evaluation. Those are natural extensions of the present thesis. The completed study here already establishes its main point: early diffusion is a strong tool for reach-oriented prioritization and a much weaker, though still potentially useful, tool for early misinformation-risk screening.
